	We would like to thank the referee for a careful and insightful analysis of our paper, and for the many suggestions improving its presentation.
	Since the report was received, the article has undergone a substantial further revision, which we summarize in \cref{s:overview} before addressing each of the referee's items; all section, lemma, and theorem numbers below refer to the current manuscript.
	We copy the report for completeness.

	\section{Reviewer's summary}

	The Steenrod squares in the cohomology of a simplicial complex were first defined by Steenrod from a collection of bilinear operations called cup-i products.
	These operations were later revisited and generalized to the odd primes in the work of McClure-Smith and Berger-Fresse. At the same time, Real gave another	very natural construction of cup-i products. More recently, Medina-Mardones	has given two new treatments to these operations: one from the viewpoint of props and another more handicrafted.
	Are all these constructions of cup-i products isomorphic? in this paper	Medina-Mardones answers in the affirmative by providing three natural-looking
	properties that characterize the cup-i constructions up to isomorphism (just as	Steenrod squares are characterized by four axioms).
	The article is well-written and clear.
	Nonetheless, there are some comments that need to be addressed by the author.

	\section{Overview of the revision}\label{s:overview}

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item The main theorem is now stated and proved for \emph{semi-simplicial} \mbox{cup-$i$} constructions, with the simplicial case as a consequence, and the third axiom was weakened from non-degenerate ($x \cup_0 x \neq 0$ for $0$-simplices) to non-zero (not identically zero).
		Both changes strengthen the characterization.

		\item The proof was restructured around a new device, the \emph{partition chains} of Section~5, which we believe yields a shorter and more transparent argument.
		The consequences of the irreducibility and freeness axioms are now developed in dedicated sections (Sections~5 and~6) before the proof of the main theorem (Section~7).

		\item The revision contains a new theorem (Theorem~5.8): every irreducible semi-simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction is automatically simplicial, so the semi-simplicial and simplicial classification problems coincide.

		\item Following the referee's suggestion (item~14 below), the revision contains a new section (Section~8) proving that the axioms of the main theorem are independent.
	\end{enumerate}

	\section{Reviewer's individual items}

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Definition 4: the operation \(\cup_i\) is defined on cochains, but in the definition it is applied to chains.

		\ar The required identification is now stated immediately before it is used in the axioms: ``Below we identify simplices with their associated chain and cochain basis elements'' (Section~2.2).

		\item Section 3.1, first paragraph: that is natural \(\rightarrow\) that are natural.

		\ar The sentence was rewritten; in the current text the relative clause unambiguously modifies the (singular) product structure.

		\item Lemma 14: ``from either V or W'': it is not enough clear that either they are missing from V or they are missing from W.

		\ar The passage was reworked during the restructuring: the action of the codegeneracy maps is now given by the explicit formula~(3), which includes the induced map on subsets, and the relevant condition appears unambiguously in the proofs of Theorem~4.2 and Theorem~5.8.

		\item Lemma 17: ``an element'': an element of which set precisely?

		\ar The lemma was restated with all objects explicitly typed.
		It is now Lemma~6.1, whose hypotheses declare the basis elements involved and the space they belong to.

		\item Lemma 17: the sudden appearance of \(\Lambda(i, n)\) is slightly disturbing for the reader, who has not seen it defined before.

		\ar The indexing set is now introduced where it first appears: ``where $\Lambda(i,n)$ is a finite (possibly empty) indexing set, and $V_\lambda \ot W_\lambda$ is a basis element for each $\lambda \in \Lambda(i,n)$'' (Section~3.6).

		\item Lemma 17, proof of (1): how is naturality used to prove the statement?

		\ar The argument was replaced.
		The corresponding statement is now part of the case $i \geq n$ of the main proof (Section~7.1), whose self-contained argument makes no implicit appeal to naturality.

		\item Lemma 19: is the partition unique?

		\ar In the current formulation the choice is parameterized by a function $\xi$ whose uniqueness is part of the statement (Lemma~6.1).

		\item Lemma 20: at this point the reader might not remember that \(\Delta\) is the canonical cup product, while \(\triangle\) is the one being inspected. In fact, he might not have noticed that a new symbol was introduced in Lemma 16.

		\ar The notation was changed to prevent this confusion: the canonical construction is now denoted $\canonical$ throughout, and the statements of Section~7 display it explicitly.

		\item Section 4.7: there is an \(r\) that has not been declared before.

		\ar It was a typo for $k$ and was corrected; no undeclared symbols remain in the current text.

		\item Section 5.1, first paragraph: Lemma 8 or Lemma 9?

		\ar It was both; this was corrected in the previous revision, and the passage has since been rewritten.

		\item Theorem 28 is not a side theorem of the paper: it is mentioned in the abstract. Therefore, it deserves a proper proof: part of the justification of the main theorem of the article is that it allows a simpler proof of the equivalence of Steenrod's and Real's constructions than a direct comparison. But that is not apparent with the current writing.

		\ar A complete proof is now given.
		The theorem in Section~9.1 verifies the axioms for Steenrod's construction -- non-vanishing, irreducibility, and freeness, the latter through an induction on the cut points of the summands -- and concludes with a tie-breaking comparison on $\simplex^{i+1}$.
		The same treatment is given to Real's construction in Section~9.2.
		Both agreements were additionally verified by computer for all $i \leq n \leq 8$.

		\item Section 6: Recall that an \(E_\infty\)-operad is an operad with the same homology as \(Z\) whose symmetric action on each arity is free. I think an \(E_\infty\)-operad needs to be projective or free as well.

		\ar The passage containing this definition was removed when the final section was restructured, so the revised manuscript no longer makes the disputed assertion.

		\item Page 18, first paragraph: you say that the surjection operad is the image of \(U(M)\) in \(Z\).
		Isn't that the point of Appendix 1 in ``A finitely presented \(E_\infty\)-prop I: algebraic context''. If so, it would be good to quote that appendix.

		\ar The requested pinpoint citation to Appendix~1 was added in Section~9.4.

		\item Apart from these comments, here is a suggestion: it would be interesting to give examples of cup-\(i\) product constructions that fail to satisfy any of the three axioms (free, non-degenerate, and irreducible).

		\ar This suggestion is now fully implemented, and became one of the contributions of the revised article.
		Section~8 proves that the three axioms are independent by exhibiting, for each of them, a simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction satisfying the other two that is not isomorphic to the canonical one: the zero construction for the non-zero axiom, the construction $(1+T)\canonical$ for freeness, and an explicit modification of the canonical construction for irreducibility.
		We remark that the non-degeneracy axiom in the version reviewed by the referee has been replaced by the weaker non-zero axiom (see \cref{s:overview}); establishing the independence of freeness under the stronger non-degeneracy axiom requires a considerably more elaborate construction, which we chose not to include in the article.
	\end{enumerate}

	\section{Additional corrections}\label{s:additional}

	\begin{enumerate}
		\item In the action of codegeneracies on the functor $\cP$, formula~(3), the induced relabeling of subsets was previously implicit and is now explicit.

		\item In the unravelled formulas for Real's construction (Section~9.2), the two displayed cases are distinguished by the parity of $i$; they were previously mislabeled by the parity of $n$.
		With this correction the case split matches, under the change of notation recorded in the accompanying footnote, the one of Gonz\'alez\-/D\'iaz\--Real.

		\item In the freeness verification for Steenrod's construction (Section~9.1), the parity assumption was aligned with the displayed identities, and the induction argument was written out in full.

		\item The case analysis of Section~7.1 was completed to exclude both types of transitions between consecutive values of $\triangle_n[n]$.

		\item The author's affiliation was updated.
	\end{enumerate}
