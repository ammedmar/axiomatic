\subsection{Diagonal and join}

The following recursive definition, introduced in \cite{medina2023dennis}, recovers Steenrod's cup-\(i\) coproducts:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prop cup-i}
	\begin{split}
		& \triangle^\rS_0 = \triangle_{\text{AW}}, \\
		& \triangle^\rS_i =
		(\ast \ot \id) \circ (\id \ot T \canonical_{i-1}) \circ \triangle_{\text{AW}}.
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
It does so with integer coefficients with the \defn{algebraic join} defined by
\begin{multline}
	\ast \big(\left[v_0, \dots, v_p \right] \ot \left[v_{p+1}, \dots, v_q\right]\big) =\\
	\begin{cases}
		(-1)^p \sign \pi \left[v_{\pi(0)}, \dots, v_{\pi(q)}\right] &
		\text{ if } v_i \neq v_j \text{ for } i \neq j, \\
		\hfil 0 &
		\text{ if not},
	\end{cases}
\end{multline}
where $\pi$ is the permutation that orders the vertices.

As proved in \cite{medina2020prop1}, arbitrary composites of the Alexander--Whitney diagonal, the join and permutations of factors assemble into a natural $E_\infty$-coalgebra structure on simplicial (integral) chains, a structure induced from a cellular analogue \cite{medina2021prop2}.

\subsection{Surjections}

Set
\[
\Delta^{[1]} = \id, \qquad \Delta^{[m+1]} = (\Delta^{[m]} \ot \id) \circ \Delta,
\qquad
\ast^{[1]} = \id, \qquad \ast^{[m+1]} = \ast \circ (\ast^{[m]} \ot \id).
\]
Over $\Ftwo$, the coassociativity of $\Delta$ and associativity of $\ast$ make the chosen parenthesizations immaterial.

Let
\[
s \colon \set{1, \dots, \ell} \twoheadrightarrow \set{1, \dots, r}
\]
be a surjection, represented by the sequence $\big(s(1), \dots, s(\ell)\big)$.
Its action on simplicial chains is the natural transformation
\begin{equation}\label{e:surjection action}
	\left(\ast^{[\bars{s^{-1}(1)}]} \ot \dotsb \ot \ast^{[\bars{s^{-1}(r)}]}\right)
	\circ \pi_s \circ \Delta^{[\ell]},
\end{equation}
where $\pi_s$ is the Koszul-signed permutation that groups the tensor factors according to the fibers of $s$, preserving their order within each fiber.
These operations define the standard action of the McClure--Smith surjection operad $\cX$ \cite{mcclure2003multivariable, medina2020prop1}.

With $\Ftwo$-coefficients, the alternating surjections
\[
(1,2), \qquad (1,2,1), \qquad (1,2,1,2), \qquad \dots
\]
define Steenrod's original simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction.
Moreover, $\cX(2)$ is isomorphic to $W$, so its bases are in bijection with the simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} constructions satisfying our axioms.

\subsection{Barratt--Eccles}

Let $G$ be a finite group, for us it will be a symmetric group, and consider the simplicial set
\[
\begin{split}
	EG_m &= \big\{ (g_0, \dots, g_m) \mid g_i \in G \big\}, \\
	\face_j(g_0, \dots, g_m) &= (g_0, \dots, \widehat g_j, \dots, g_m), \\
	\dege_j(g_0, \dots, g_m) &= (g_0, \dots, g_j, g_j, \dots, g_m).
\end{split}
\]
The partial composition of permutations
\[
\circ_j \colon \Sym_p \times \Sym_q \to \Sym_{p+q-1}
\]
induces an operad structure on
\[
\cE = \set{\chains(E\Sym_r)}_{r \geq 1},
\]
the \defn{Barratt--Eccles operad} \cite{berger2004combinatorial}.
Berger and Fresse constructed a natural $\cE$-coalgebra structure on simplicial chains.
Over $\Ftwo$, this action factors through the surjection-operad action by
\[
\cE \xra{\TR} \cX,
\]
where $\TR$ is an isomorphism in arity~$2$.
Consequently, the elements
\[
\big\{(\id, T, \id, \dots, T^i)\big\}_{i \in \N}
\]
define a simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction agreeing with Steenrod's.
Since $\cE(2) \cong W$, its bases are likewise in bijection with the simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} constructions satisfying our axioms.

We refer to \cite{medina2021comch} for a computer algebra system implementing these combinatorial operads.
