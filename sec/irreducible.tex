\section{Irreducibility}\label{s:irreducible}

In this section, we study the irreducibility condition.
It will be convenient to work in the \textbf{augmented simplex category}, which contains an additional object \([-1] = \emptyset\) and a unique morphism from it to every \([n]\).
The natural equivalence between $\cP$ and $\chains$ of \cref{st:chains_functor_equivalence} extends to this category; we denote the extended functors by $\widehat{\cP}$ and $\widehat\chains$, respectively.
For every $n$, the only additional basis element of $\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)$ is the full subset $\set{0,\dots,n}$ in degree $-1$.
We identify the underlying graded vector spaces of $\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)$ and $\cP(\simplex^n)$ in non-negative degrees.

The main results of this section are an explicit description of the largest subcomplex of irreducible augmented chains and a proof that irreducibility together with semi-simpliciality implies simpliciality.

\subsection{Irreducible and partition chains}

A basis element $V \ot W \in \widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}$ is said to be \textbf{irreducible} if $V \cap W = \emptyset$ and \textbf{reducible} otherwise.
We denote by $\pired$ and $\piirred$ the projections onto the graded subspaces generated by reducible and irreducible basis elements, respectively.
Under the identification above, a cup-$i$ construction $\triangle$ is irreducible if and only if its defining elements $\triangle_i[n]$ are irreducible in this sense for all $i,n \in \N$.

The graded subspace generated by reducible basis elements is a subcomplex, whereas the subspace generated by irreducible basis elements is not.
Nonetheless, we can explicitly describe the largest subcomplex contained in the irreducible graded subspace.
For any $U \subseteq \set{0,\dots,n}$, its \textbf{partition chain} is the element
\[
\zeta_U = \sum V \ot W
\]
in \(\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}\), where the sum is taken over all basis elements \(V \ot W\) satisfying \(V \union W = U\) and \(V \cap W = \emptyset\).
Partition chains are irreducible.
If $U\subsetneq\set{0,\dots,n}$, then $\zeta_U$ is concentrated in non-negative bidegrees and agrees with its image in the ordinary complex \(\cP(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}\).

\begin{theorem}\label{t:augmented_partition_subcomplex}
	Partition chains span the largest subcomplex contained in the irreducible subspace of $\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	Let $I$ be the irreducible subspace of $\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}$ and let $A$ be the span of the partition chains.
	We first prove that
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:augmented_irreducible_kernel}
		A = I \cap \bd^{-1}(I).
	\end{equation}
	Let $\zeta = \sum_\Lambda V \ot W$ be an irreducible chain, where $\Lambda$ is a set of distinct irreducible basis elements.
	The reducible part of its boundary is
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:reducible_augmented_boundary}
		\pired(\bd\zeta) =
		\sum_\Lambda\left(
		\sum_{w \in W} w.V \ot W +
		\sum_{v \in V} V \ot v.W
		\right).
	\end{equation}
	No two terms in the first family agree, and no two terms in the second family agree.
	Moreover, a term from the first inner sum agrees with one from the second precisely when the corresponding partitions differ by moving the common element from one factor to the other.
	Consequently, \cref{eq:reducible_augmented_boundary} vanishes if and only if $\Lambda$ is closed under every such basic move.

	Any two partitions of a fixed set $U$ are related by a sequence of basic moves, while a basic move does not change their union.
	It follows that $\Lambda$ is closed under basic moves if and only if, for each set $U$, it contains either every partition of $U$ or none of them.
	This proves \cref{eq:augmented_irreducible_kernel}.

	If $x \in A$, then \cref{eq:augmented_irreducible_kernel} gives $\bd x \in I$.
	Since $\bd^2x = 0 \in I$, applying the same identity to $\bd x$ shows that $\bd x \in A$.
	Thus, $A$ is a subcomplex.
	Finally, if $K$ is any subcomplex contained in $I$ and $x \in K$, then both $x$ and $\bd x$ belong to $I$.
	Equation~\eqref{eq:augmented_irreducible_kernel} therefore gives $x \in A$, so $K \subseteq A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{c:boundary_partition_chain}
	For ever $U \subseteq \set{0,\dots,n}$ in \(\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}\) we have
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundary_partition_chain}
		\bd\zeta_U = \sum_{\bar u \notin U}\zeta_{\bar u.U}.
	\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
	By \cref{t:augmented_partition_subcomplex}, the reducible terms in $\bd\zeta_U$ cancel.
	The remaining terms add an element $\bar u \notin U$ to one of the two factors.
	For each such $\bar u$, every partition of $\bar u.U$ occurs exactly once, according to the factor containing $\bar u$.
	This proves \cref{eq:boundary_partition_chain} in the augmented complex.
\end{proof}

The following comparison with the ordinary complex is the form needed later.

\begin{corollary}\label{l:partition chains}
	Let $\zeta \in \cP(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}$ be a homogeneous irreducible chain of degree at least $n$.
	Then $\pired(\bd\zeta) = 0$ if and only if $\zeta$ is a sum of partition chains.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
	View $\zeta$ as a graded chain in $\widehat{\cP}(\simplex^n)^{\ot 2}$.
	The difference between its augmented and ordinary boundaries consists of terms obtained by applying the augmentation to a degree-zero tensor factor.
	If such a term occurs, the degree assumption forces the other factor to have degree $n$.
	The resulting basis element is therefore of the form $\set{0,\dots,n} \ot \emptyset$ or $\emptyset \ot \set{0,\dots,n}$ and is irreducible.
	Consequently, the reducible parts of the augmented and ordinary boundaries agree.

	By \cref{eq:augmented_irreducible_kernel}, the vanishing of this common reducible part is equivalent to $\zeta$ being a sum of partition chains in the augmented complex.
	If $\zeta$ has degree $d \geq n$, every such partition chain is indexed by a set $U$ of cardinality $2n-d \leq n$ and therefore contains no degree $-1$ factor.
	It therefore agrees with its image in the ordinary complex, proving the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Irreducibility implies simpliciality}

\begin{theorem}\label{t:irreducible_implies_simplicial}
	Every irreducible semi-simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction is simplicial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
	Let $\triangle$ be an irreducible semi-simplicial \mbox{cup-$i$} construction, and fix $i,n \in \N$ with $n > 0$.
	We use the coefficient notation $\coeff{\zeta}{V \ot W}$ introduced in \cref{ss:axioms_revisited}.
	Each $\triangle_i[n]$ has degree $n+i\geq n$, so the preceding comparison allows us to compute the reducible part of its boundary in the augmented complex while retaining the ordinary cup-$i$ identities.

	We first claim that
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:reducible_part}
		\pired\big(\bd\triangle_i[n]\big) = \triangle_i\bd\,[n].
	\end{equation}
	Indeed, $\triangle_i\bd\,[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \cP(\delta_k)^{\ot 2}\triangle_i[n-1]$ and, since every subset in the image of $\cP(\delta_k)$ contains $k$, every basis element in the image of $\cP(\delta_k)^{\ot 2}$ is reducible.
	On the other hand, $(1+T)\triangle_{i-1}[n]$ is a sum of irreducible basis elements.
	Applying $\pired$ to the identity $\bd\triangle_i[n] + \triangle_i\bd\,[n] = (1+T)\triangle_{i-1}[n]$ proves \eqref{eq:reducible_part}.

	Let us compare coefficients on both sides of \eqref{eq:reducible_part}.
	Writing $\triangle_i[n] = \sum_\Lambda V \ot W$, the reducible part of its boundary is
	\[
	\pired\big(\bd\triangle_i[n]\big) =
	\sum_{\Lambda}\Big(\sum_{w \in W} w.V \ot W + \sum_{v \in V} V \ot v.W\Big),
	\]
	so every basis element appearing on either side of \cref{eq:reducible_part} is of the form $X \ot Y$ with $X \cap Y = \set{k}$ for exactly one $k$.
	Fix such an $X \ot Y$.
	Its coefficient on the left-hand side is
	\[
	\coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{X \setminus k \ot Y} + \coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{X \ot Y \setminus k}.
	\]
	For $0 \leq k \leq n$, let $\rho_k$ denote the order-preserving relabeling from $\set{0,\dots,n} \setminus \set{k}$ to $\set{0,\dots,n-1}$; explicitly, $\rho_k(u) = u$ for $u < k$ and $\rho_k(u) = u-1$ for $u > k$, extended elementwise to subsets.
	For the right-hand side, notice that $\cP(\delta_m)^{\ot 2}(A \ot B) = X \ot Y$ forces $m = k$, and that $\cP(\delta_k)$ defines a bijection from the subsets of $\set{0,\dots,n-1}$ to the subsets of $\set{0,\dots,n}$ containing $k$, with inverse $S \mapsto \rho_k(S \setminus k)$.
	Therefore, \cref{eq:reducible_part} is equivalent to the identity
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient_comparison}
		\coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{X \setminus k \ot Y} + \coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{X \ot Y \setminus k} =
		\coeff{\triangle_i[n-1]}{\rho_k(X \setminus k) \ot \rho_k(Y \setminus k)}
	\end{equation}
	holding for every basis element $X \ot Y$ with $X \cap Y = \set{k}$.

	By \cref{l:simplicial_from_semisimplicial}, it suffices to show that $\cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}\triangle_i[n] = 0$ for every codegeneracy $\sigma_j \colon [n] \to [n-1]$.
	Inspecting \cref{eq:codegeneracies}, an irreducible basis element $V \ot W$ satisfies $\cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}(V \ot W) \neq 0$ if and only if one of $j,j+1$ belongs to $V$ and the other belongs to $W$.
	For such an element, let $V' \ot W'$ be the irreducible basis element obtained by exchanging $j$ and $j+1$; explicitly, if $j \in V$ and $j+1 \in W$, then $V' = (V \setminus j) \union \set{j{+}1}$ and $W' = (W \setminus j{+}1) \union \set{j}$.
	A direct verification using \cref{eq:codegeneracies} shows that
	\[
	\cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}(V \ot W) = \cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}(V' \ot W')
	\]
	and that $V \ot W$ and $V' \ot W'$ are the only irreducible basis elements with this image that are not annihilated by $\cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}$.
	Consequently, since $\triangle_i[n]$ is irreducible, $\cP(\sigma_j)^{\ot 2}\triangle_i[n] = 0$ if and only if
	\begin{equation}\label{eq:swap}
		\coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V \ot W} = \coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V' \ot W'}
	\end{equation}
	for every such pair.

	To establish \cref{eq:swap}, assume that $j \in V$ and $j+1 \in W$, the other case being symmetric.
	Applying \cref{eq:coefficient_comparison} to the basis element $V \ot \big(W \union \set{j}\big)$, whose factors intersect in $\set{j}$, we obtain
	\[
	\coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V \setminus j \ot W \union \set{j}} + \coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V \ot W} =
	\coeff{\triangle_i[n-1]}{\rho_j(V \setminus j) \ot \rho_j(W)},
	\]
	and applying it to $V' \ot \big(W \union \set{j}\big)$, whose factors intersect in $\set{j+1}$, we obtain
	\begin{multline}
		\coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V \setminus j \ot W \union \set{j}} + \coeff{\triangle_i[n]}{V' \ot W'} = \\
		\coeff{\triangle_i[n-1]}{\rho_{j+1}(V \setminus j) \ot \rho_{j+1}\big((W \setminus j{+}1) \union \set{j}\big)}.
	\end{multline}
	Since $V \setminus j$ contains neither $j$ nor $j+1$, we have $\rho_j(V \setminus j) = \rho_{j+1}(V \setminus j)$, and direct inspection gives $\rho_j(W) = \rho_{j+1}\big((W \setminus j{+}1) \union \set{j}\big)$.
	The right-hand sides of the two identities above therefore agree, and adding them yields \cref{eq:swap}.
\end{proof}
